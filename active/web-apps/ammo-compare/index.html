Ammo Comparison Table

Every cartridge in Wikipedia’s handgun and rifle table, plus shotgun loads and current-production rounds. Each row carries the cartridge’s origin, age, and bullet dimensions; energy and power factor are computed from bullet weight and muzzle velocity, not copied from a chart.

Comparison of typical ammunition loads by introduction year, bullet weight, velocity, energy, power factor, recoil, and cost, with a silhouette drawn to a shared scale.
Profile

How to read this. Curated rows are typical commercial loads from everyday barrel lengths (pistol ≈ 4″, rifle ≈ 22–24″, shotgun ≈ 28″), so a hotter or slower load of the same cartridge will read differently. The extended reference rows come from Wikipedia’s cartridge table, which lists independent maxima for velocity and energy; their bullet weight is derived from the published momentum so each row describes one coherent load. Energy is gr × fps² / 450437 foot-pounds; power factor is gr × fps / 1000. Energy bars use a logarithmic scale so rimfire and anti-materiel rounds can share one column. Shotgun rows use the whole payload, so their power factor is not comparable to a single projectile.

Cost per round is shown only for cartridges with a current commercial load and is a rough retail estimate for relative comparison only. Obsolete, surplus, and collector cartridges have no stable price, so their cost is left blank rather than invented. “Introduced” is the year the cartridge was introduced; the line under each name gives its nation, bullet diameter, and case length. Bullet cells show the selected unit plus, where known, the range of weights normally sold for that cartridge. Click a row for platforms, reference metadata, and notes. The silhouette beside each load is drawn from its case length, bullet diameter, and bullet weight; sizes use a compressed scale so small rounds stay legible, while the true dimensions are printed in the row.
