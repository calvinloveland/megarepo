Common centerfire, rimfire, and shotgun loads side by side. Energy and power factor are computed from bullet weight and muzzle velocity, not copied from a chart, so the math always agrees with the numbers in the row.

Comparison of typical ammunition loads by bullet weight, velocity, energy, power factor, recoil, and cost.

How to read this. Values are typical commercial loads from everyday barrel lengths (pistol ≈ 4″, rifle ≈ 22–24″, shotgun ≈ 28″), so a hotter or slower load of the same cartridge will read differently. Energy is gr × fps² / 450437 foot-pounds; power factor is gr × fps / 1000. Shotgun rows use the whole payload, so their power factor is not comparable to a single projectile.

Cost per round is a rough retail estimate for relative comparison only. Click a row for platforms and notes.
